What this does not tell you

This model is built around street photos. It is not a GPS replacement. Distance from a known location measures error, not confidence. A changed estimate is not proof of privacy; visual clues can remain after editing.

What leaves your browser

Metadata inspection, covers, crops and downloads happen locally. Live analysis sends only re-encoded pixels to server memory, without upload storage. Street map tiles are optional: enabling them contacts OpenStreetMap and reveals your IP and map area.

Recorded versus live pixels

Examples are quality-90 JPEG display exports of evaluation images. Historical predictions were made on different source pixels. Live edit comparisons reanalyze the normalized display original and its edit, and require matching model settings. A chosen cover is an illustration, not aggregate evidence.

Research: Chiikabu Labs Chipoint v2, SigLIP2 / OpenCLIP, Meta DINOv3 and OSV-5M.

Look. Reveal. Rethink.

  1. Start with a photo.

    Use a recorded street scene, or choose an image you have permission to analyze. Original metadata is inspected locally; GPS tags are not used for inference.

  2. Reveal an estimate.

    Examples show a saved result, clearly marked as recorded. Your own photos require live inference. A separate reveal shows the known location of an example.

  3. Change what’s visible.

    Cover a rectangular detail or apply a crop. With live inference, test the edited pixels and compare estimates. Without it, you can still edit and download a metadata-free PNG.

An estimate can be wrong. Removing metadata does not remove visual location clues.
